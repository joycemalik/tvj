Abstract
We present an automated spectral fitting pipeline for the UV emission lines (Ly\(\beta\)+O VI, Ly\(\alpha\), N V, O I+Si II, C II, Si IV+O IV], C IV, He II, O III]) in 255 archival International Ultraviolet Explorer (IUE) Short-Wavelength Prime (SWP) low-dispersion spectra of the quasar 3C 273. The continuum is a power law fitted by log–log least squares in five line-free windows. Each line is located on a coarse grid and then fitted by weighted least squares as a Gaussian plus a local linear continuum (overlapping lines jointly), with DER_SNR pixel noise and covariance-based uncertainties. Every fit is verified with a goodness-of-fit test corrected for pixel correlation, a runs test, a normality test and a model-independent flux check. Line variability is quantified by the fractional variability amplitude \(F_\text{var}\) computed per line and per calendar year (Vaughan et al. 2003). This document describes every computational step and the literature it rests on.
§ 1

Introduction

The quasar 3C 273 (RA 12h 29m 06.7s, Dec +02° 03′ 09″; redshift \(z = 0.158\)) is among the brightest and most studied quasi-stellar objects in the sky. Its UV continuum and broad emission lines have been monitored extensively with the International Ultraviolet Explorer (IUE) satellite, which operated from 1978 to 1996 and accumulated a uniquely dense, long-baseline UV spectral archive for this source.[1,2]

The Short-Wavelength Prime (SWP) camera covers 1150–1975 Å in low-dispersion mode at a resolving power of \(R \approx 300\) (\(\Delta\lambda \approx 4\)–6 Å), capturing Lyman-Alpha at its redshifted observed wavelength \(\lambda_\text{obs} \approx 1408\) Å. The campaign files have been divided by \(1+z\), so this pipeline works in the rest frame and the line appears near 1213–1218 Å.

Measuring Ly\(\alpha\) flux accurately is critical for: (a) reverberation mapping of the broad-line region (BLR), (b) variability analysis of the accretion disc, and (c) quantifying the relationship between continuum and emission-line fluxes on year-to-decade timescales.[4,5] Automated, reproducible fitting across all 255 SWP spectra requires a pipeline robust to varying S/N, continuum slope, and line asymmetry.

§ 2

End-to-End Pipeline Overview

The pipeline is implemented in Python and exposed through a Flask web application. Each spectrum file (two-column ASCII: wavelength in Å, flux in erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² Å⁻¹) is processed independently. The eight principal stages are:

1
Data Ingestion & Parsing
Read 2-column ASCII spectrum; validate wavelength monotonicity and positive flux.
2
Power-Law Continuum Modeling
Fit \(F_c(\lambda) = A\,\lambda^\alpha\) via log–log ordinary least squares on line-free windows. Subtract to obtain \(F_\text{sub}(\lambda) = F(\lambda) - F_c(\lambda)\).
3
Observed Peak Location
For each spectrum and each line, locate the brightest pixel of the smoothed continuum-subtracted spectrum within a per-line radius of the rest wavelength (Ly\(\alpha\) ±15 Å, Ly\(\beta\) ±10 Å, others 10–15 Å), skipping masked pixels such as geocoronal Ly\(\alpha\) airglow at 1049.8 Å. This per-spectrum peak \(\lambda_\text{peak}\) anchors the center grid, so the starting position follows each spectrum.
4
Seed Grid Search
Evaluate Gaussians on a coarse \((\mu, \sigma, W)\) grid inside the per-line ranges (§5) and rank them with the composite score (§6). The best-ranked candidate is only a starting point.
5
Weighted Least-Squares Refinement
Fit Gaussian(s) plus a local linear continuum to the observed flux with scipy.optimize.least_squares (§7). Overlapping lines (Ly\(\alpha\) + N V) are fitted together. Parameter uncertainties come from the covariance matrix.
6
Physical Statistics
Line flux \(\sqrt{2\pi}A\sigma\) with propagated error, equivalent width, FWHM (observed and instrument-corrected), significance and \(\chi^2_\text{red}\) (§8).
7
Detection & Verification
A line is detected when \(F/\sigma_F \ge 3\). Every refined fit is then verified with goodness-of-fit, residual randomness, residual normality and a model-independent flux check (§9).
8
Variability
Detected fluxes are joined to observation dates and \(F_\text{var}\) is computed per line per calendar year (§10).
§ 3

Data Ingestion & Pre-processing

Each IUE SWP low-dispersion spectrum is a two-column text file: wavelength \(\lambda\) (Å) and flux density \(F_\lambda\) (erg s\(^{-1}\) cm\(^{-2}\) Å\(^{-1}\)). The 255 campaign files are rest-frame spectra: they were divided by \(1+z\) with \(z = 0.158\) for 3C 273. Two facts confirm this: the pixel spacing is \(1.448\) Å \(= 1.676/(1+z)\) Å, where 1.676 Å is the SWP MXLO sampling in the observed frame, and geocoronal Ly\(\alpha\) airglow (1215.67 Å observed) appears at \(1215.67/1.158 = 1049.8\) Å.

Usable data run from 993.6 Å to ≈1832 Å (pixels with zero flux at the blue edge are ignored). No rebinning is applied. Observation dates (JD) are taken from the SWP log jd.xlsx; five spectra (251–255) have no recorded date.

File convention: Spectrum files follow the IUE MAST naming scheme, e.g., 70dcdr2dswp35476mxlo.txt, where 70 is the campaign sequence number and swp35476 is the SWP image number.
§ 4

Continuum Modeling: Power-Law Fit

The UV continuum of a quasar/AGN is well described by a power law \(F_c(\lambda) = A\,\lambda^\alpha\), where \(A\) is the continuum amplitude and \(\alpha\) is the spectral index (typically \(\alpha \approx -1.5\) to \(-2.5\) for UV continua).[6] Taking the natural logarithm linearises the problem:

(Eq. 1) \[\ln F_c = \alpha \ln\lambda + \ln A\]

Ordinary least squares is applied to \((\ln\lambda_i, \ln F_i)\) for all pixels in five line-free rest-frame windows: 1100–1150, 1325–1370, 1425–1475, 1590–1620 and 1660–1700 Å. These are the windows of the campaign tables; with them the spectral indices reproduce the tabulated values for all 255 spectra (maximum difference \(5\times10^{-5}\)). The 1325–1370 Å window contains the weak C II λ1335 blend, which is undetected in most epochs.[7]

Least-Squares Solution

Let \(x_i = \ln\lambda_i\) and \(y_i = \ln F_i\) for \(n\) continuum pixels. The OLS estimators are:

(Eq. 2) \[ \hat{\alpha} = \frac{n\sum x_i y_i - (\sum x_i)(\sum y_i)}{n\sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}, \qquad \hat{A} = \exp\!\left(\frac{\sum y_i - \hat{\alpha}\sum x_i}{n}\right) \]

The best-fit continuum \(\hat{F}_c(\lambda) = \hat{A}\,\lambda^{\hat{\alpha}}\) is then evaluated on the full wavelength grid, and the continuum-subtracted (emission-only) spectrum is:

(Eq. 3) \[F_\text{sub}(\lambda) = F(\lambda) - \hat{F}_c(\lambda)\]

Uncertainties

With residual variance \(s^2 = \sum (y_i - \hat\alpha x_i - \ln\hat A)^2/(n-2)\) and \(S_{xx} = \sum (x_i - \bar x)^2\), the standard errors of the OLS estimators are[10]

(Eq. 3a) \[ \sigma_{\hat\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{s^2}{S_{xx}}}, \qquad \sigma_{\ln\hat A} = \sqrt{s^2\left(\frac{1}{n} + \frac{\bar x^2}{S_{xx}}\right)}, \qquad \sigma_{\hat A} = \hat A\,\sigma_{\ln\hat A}. \]

The campaign tables list \(\sigma_A\) values that are \(\sqrt{n}\) (≈12) times larger, consistent with the \(1/n\) term being omitted; the pipeline reports Eq. 3a. For Spectrum 70: \(\hat\alpha = -0.933 \pm 0.055\), \(\hat A = (1.57 \pm 0.62)\times10^{-10}\).

§ 5

Stage A: Discrete Candidate Grid Search

5.1 Design Philosophy

Rather than feeding a continuous solver a single starting point and trusting it to find the global minimum, the candidate engine takes a decision-making approach: enumerate a physically constrained set of Gaussian models and rank them by a multi-criteria score that mirrors how a human expert would judge a fit. This avoids local-minimum traps common in UV emission-line fitting where low S/N or asymmetric profiles create false optima.[8]

5.2 Gaussian Emission-Line Model

Each candidate emission-line model is a single Gaussian:

(Eq. 4) \[ G(\lambda;\,A,\mu,\sigma) = A \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\lambda - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \]

where \(A\) is the peak amplitude (erg s\(^{-1}\) cm\(^{-2}\) Å\(^{-1}\)), \(\mu\) is the line center (Å), and \(\sigma\) is the Gaussian width (Å).

5.3 Parameter Grids

The search space is defined over three parameters:

ParameterGrid DefinitionTypical SizeRationale
Center \(\mu\) \(\lambda_\text{peak} \pm\) range (Ly\(\alpha\): ±5 Å), step 0.25–0.5 Å per line Anchored to this spectrum's observed peak; refined continuously within ±8 Å of rest
Width \(\sigma\) Continuous range \([\sigma_\text{min}, \sigma_\text{max}]\); Ly\(\alpha\): 4.25–10.19 Å bounds Ly\(\alpha\) FWHM 10–24 Å (\(\sigma = \text{FWHM}/2\sqrt{2\ln 2}\)); approved fits 10.6–20.0 Å; floor = IUE resolution

Wing window \(W\) Range 10–24 Å (Ly\(\alpha\)), step 1 Å 15 values Full width of the reference-style integration window; approved windows 13–23 Å

Why these ranges. A Gaussian has FWHM \(= 2\sqrt{2\ln 2}\,\sigma \approx 2.3548\,\sigma\), so the Ly\(\alpha\) FWHM range of 10–24 Å corresponds to \(\sigma = 4.25\)–10.19 Å (2500–5900 km s\(^{-1}\) at 1216 Å). This brackets the approved fits (FWHM 10.6–20.0 Å, 2600–4950 km s\(^{-1}\)) and published broad-line widths of 3C 273 (Paltani & Türler 2005). No line can appear narrower than the instrument: the IUE SWP low-dispersion resolution is ≈6 Å FWHM observed, i.e. 6/(1+z) = 5.2 Å in these de-redshifted spectra (z = 0.158), giving the floor \(\sigma \ge 2.2\) Å used for the weaker lines. The grid only seeds the fit: \(\sigma\) may take any value between its bounds in the least-squares refinement.

5.4 Noise Estimation (Robust MAD)

A robust noise estimate is required both for scoring (\(\chi^2\)) and for SNR computation. The pipeline uses the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD), scaled to match the standard deviation of a normal distribution:[9]

(Eq. 5) \[ \hat{\sigma}_\text{noise} = 1.4826 \times \text{median}\!\left(\,|r_i - \tilde{r}|\,\right), \quad \tilde{r} = \text{median}(r_i) \]

where \(r_i = F_\text{sub}(\lambda_i) / \bar{F}_c\) are the normalised continuum-subtracted residuals over a ±20 Å window around the line. The factor 1.4826 converts the MAD to a Gaussian-equivalent \(\sigma\). The absolute noise level is then \(\sigma_n = \hat{\sigma}_\text{noise}\,|\bar{F}_c|\).

§ 6

Composite Scoring Function (seed selection)

Each grid candidate \((\mu, \sigma, W)\), with its amplitude from linear least squares, receives a composite score \(S \in [0, 1]\). The score only chooses the starting point of the least-squares refinement (§7); the reported parameters and errors come from that fit. Weights as implemented in src/candidate_engine.py:

(Eq. 6) \[ S = 0.35\,s_{\chi^2} + 0.20\,s_\text{peak} + 0.15\,s_\text{left} + 0.10\,s_\text{right} + 0.12\,s_W + 0.08\,s_\sigma \]
CriterionWeightComponent FormulaRationale
Reduced \(\chi^2\)0.35 \(s_{\chi^2} = \exp\!\bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\max(\chi^2_\text{red}-1,\,0)}{1.5}\bigr)^2\bigr]\) Only a poor fit (\(\chi^2_\text{red} > 1\)) is penalised; a low \(\chi^2_\text{red}\) is accepted
Peak alignment0.20 \(s_\text{peak} = 0.6\,e^{-(\mu-\lambda_\text{peak})^2/(2\cdot3^2)} + 0.4\,e^{-(\mu-\lambda_0)^2/(2\cdot5^2)}\) Towards this spectrum's observed peak and the rest wavelength \(\lambda_0\)
Blue-side residuals0.15 \(s_\text{left} = \exp\!\bigl[-\sqrt{\langle r_\text{left}^2\rangle}/\sigma_n\bigr]\) RMS residual blueward of \(\mu\), in units of the noise
Red-side residuals0.10 \(s_\text{right} = \exp\!\bigl[-\sqrt{\langle r_\text{right}^2\rangle}/\sigma_n\bigr]\) As above, redward of \(\mu\)
Window prior0.12 \(s_W = \exp[-\tfrac12((W - W_0)/\sigma_W)^2]\) Soft preference inside the configured window range (Ly\(\alpha\): \(W_0 = 18\), \(\sigma_W = 2.5\) Å)

There is no penalty for a low \(\chi^2_\text{red}\): with the coarse IUE sampling, values well below 1 occur for legitimate fits (approved fits reach 0.05).

§ 7

Stage B: Weighted Least-Squares Refinement

The grid candidate seeds a continuous fit of the observed flux over a per-line window (e.g. 1180–1265 Å for Ly\(\alpha\) + N V, 998–1085 Å for Ly\(\beta\) + O VI, 1510–1590 Å for C IV). The model contains the line, any overlapping companion line and a local linear continuum, all fitted simultaneously:

(Eq. 8) \[ M(\lambda) = \sum_{k} A_k \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\lambda-\mu_k)^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\right] + c_0 + c_1(\lambda - \bar\lambda), \qquad \chi^2 = \sum_i \left(\frac{F_i - M(\lambda_i)}{\sigma_\text{pix}}\right)^2 . \]

\(\chi^2\) is minimised with the trust-region reflective algorithm (scipy.optimize.least_squares)[8] under bounds \(A_k \ge 0\), \(|\mu_k - \lambda_{0,k}| \le 8\) Å and \(\sigma_\text{min} \le \sigma_k \le \sigma_\text{max}\) (§5). Fluxes are fitted in units of \(10^{-13}\) so all parameters have comparable scale. Masked ranges (geocoronal airglow at 1043–1056 Å) are excluded. Fitting the continuum together with the line avoids the bias of subtracting a baseline that itself contains line flux.

7.1 Pixel noise

\(\sigma_\text{pix}\) is the DER_SNR estimate[16] \(\sigma = 1.482602/\sqrt{6}\;\text{median}\,|2F_i - F_{i-2} - F_{i+2}|\), evaluated on the fit residuals \(F_i - M(\lambda_i)\) so that the curvature of the line itself does not inflate it. Because the weights are uniform, the best-fit parameters do not depend on \(\sigma_\text{pix}\); it sets \(\chi^2\) and the uncertainties.

7.2 Uncertainties

The covariance matrix is \(C = (J^\mathsf{T}J)^{-1}\,\max(1, \chi^2_\text{red})\), where \(J\) is the Jacobian of the normalised residuals; scaling by \(\chi^2_\text{red}\) when it exceeds 1 inflates errors for imperfect models.[10]

§ 8

Physical Statistics Computation

8.1 Integrated flux and its error

(Eq. 9) \[ F = \sqrt{2\pi}\,A\,\sigma, \qquad \sigma_F^2 = 2\pi\left(\sigma^2 C_{AA} + A^2 C_{\sigma\sigma} + 2A\sigma\,C_{A\sigma}\right) \]

This is the full Gaussian flux, independent of any window. For comparison with the campaign tables, flux_window also reports the Gaussian integrated only over \([\mu - W/2, \mu + W/2]\) (the convention of the reference tool), which captures \(\operatorname{erf}\!\left(W/(2\sqrt2\,\sigma)\right)\) of the line, i.e. 72–92 % for the approved Ly\(\alpha\) windows.

8.2 Significance

(Eq. 10) \[ \text{SNR} = F / \sigma_F \]

A line is detected when \(F/\sigma_F \ge 3\).

8.3 Equivalent width

(Eq. 11) \[ W_\lambda = \frac{F}{F_c(\mu)}, \qquad \sigma_{W} = \frac{\sigma_F}{F_c(\mu)} \]

where \(F_c(\mu) = c_0 + c_1(\mu - \bar\lambda)\) is the fitted local continuum at the line centre.[11]

8.4 FWHM

(Eq. 12) \[ \text{FWHM} = 2\sqrt{2\ln 2}\,\sigma, \qquad \text{FWHM}_v = \frac{\text{FWHM}}{\mu}\,c, \qquad \text{FWHM}_\text{int} = \sqrt{\text{FWHM}^2 - \text{FWHM}_\text{inst}^2} \]

with \(\text{FWHM}_\text{inst} = 6/(1+z) = 5.18\) Å (IUE SWP low dispersion, rest frame).[14,21] Both observed and intrinsic widths are reported.

8.5 Reduced chi-squared

(Eq. 13) \[ \chi^2_\text{red} = \chi^2 / \nu, \qquad \nu = n_\text{pix} - n_\text{par} \]

\(n_\text{par}\) counts every fitted parameter (3 per Gaussian + 2 continuum terms). \(\chi^2_\text{red} \approx 1\) means the residuals match the pixel noise; a low value is not penalised. Its formal significance is tested in §9.

\(\rho\) is the lag-1 autocorrelation of the pixel noise, measured per spectrum in the line-free continuum windows (typically \(\rho \approx 0.5\), because the MXLO spectra are resampled). What the checks show. Over all 1,057 detected line fits in the 255 spectra, 86 % of Si IV + O IV] fits verify, while a single Gaussian fails the goodness-of-fit test for 65 % of the Ly\(\alpha\) and N V fits, and 37 % of C IV fits show systematic residual structure (runs test). This is expected: quasar broad emission lines are generally not Gaussian, and C IV is often asymmetric.[21] Fluxes remain the best-fit estimates with their stated errors; the model-independent flux check passes for 98.8 % of them.

§ 10

Campaign Variability Statistics

Over 250 dated IUE SWP epochs spanning 1978–1996, the emission-line fluxes of 3C 273 varies on timescales of weeks to years, driven by changes in the accretion rate and BLR response. Two standard variability metrics are computed:[3,12,13]

10.1 Fractional Variability Amplitude \(F_\text{var}\)

The fractional variability amplitude quantifies intrinsic variability relative to the measurement uncertainties:

(Eq. 18) \[ F_\text{var} = \frac{\sqrt{S^2 - \langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle}}{\bar{F}} \]

where \(S^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum(F_i - \bar{F})^2\) is the sample variance, \(\langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle = \frac{1}{N}\sum\sigma_{F,i}^2\) is the mean squared measurement error, and \(\bar{F}\) is the mean flux. When \(S^2 \le \langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle\) the scatter is consistent with the measurement errors alone and \(F_\text{var}\) is undefined ("not variable").

\(F_\text{var}\) is computed separately for every emission line and every calendar year, using the light curve \((\text{JD} - \text{JD}_\text{min},\ F,\ F_\text{err})\) of the detected (\(F/\sigma_F \ge 3\)) fluxes; observation dates come from the SWP log (jd.xlsx). Its uncertainty follows Vaughan et al. (2003) and Sukanya et al. (2018):

(Eq. 18a) \[ \mathrm{err}(F_\text{var}) \approx \begin{cases} \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2N}}\;\dfrac{\langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle}{\bar{F}^2 F_\text{var}}, & S^2/\langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle < 10 \quad (S^2 \approx \sigma_\text{err}^2)\\[2ex] \sqrt{\dfrac{\langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle}{N}}\;\dfrac{1}{\bar{F}}, & S^2/\langle\sigma_\text{err}^2\rangle \ge 10 \quad (S^2 \gg \sigma_\text{err}^2) \end{cases} \]

10.2 Flux Ratio \(R_\text{max}\)

The simplest variability measure is the ratio of maximum to minimum observed flux:

(Eq. 19) \[ R_\text{max} = \frac{F_\text{max}}{F_\text{min}} \]

For the 3C 273 Ly\(\alpha\) campaign, typical values are \(F_\text{var} \approx 0.1\)–0.3 and \(R_\text{max} \approx 2\)–4, consistent with the moderate variability amplitude characteristic of radio-loud quasars in this luminosity range.

§ 12

IUE SWP Instrument Characteristics

The Short-Wavelength Prime camera of the IUE satellite employed a Vidicon detector with a caesium telluride photocathode, covering 1150–1975 Å. In low-dispersion mode (the standard for monitoring campaigns), the spectral resolving power is \(R = \lambda/\Delta\lambda \approx 300\), corresponding to a resolution element of approximately 5–6 Å FWHM. Individual spectra are extracted and calibrated by the IUE reduction pipeline (NEWSIPS for post-1992 data, or the original IUESIPS for earlier data) to produce flux-calibrated 1-D spectra in absolute units.[14]

Two numbers matter for this pipeline. (i) Resolution: ≈6 Å FWHM in the observed frame, 5.18 Å in these rest-frame spectra; it is the lower limit for line widths and is removed in quadrature from the reported intrinsic FWHM. (ii) Sampling: 1.676 Å per MXLO pixel observed, 1.448 Å rest frame. Neighbouring pixels are correlated (lag-1 \(\rho \approx 0.5\)), which is accounted for in the goodness-of-fit test (§9.1).[14]

Sky background is negligible for bright point sources in the UV; scattered-light corrections are included in the NEWSIPS flux calibration. No additional background subtraction is applied by this pipeline.
